# Sample datasets

Four simulated datasets for exploring IRTBoss. Every one of them was generated by
[`backend/scripts/generate_sample_datasets.py`](../../backend/scripts/generate_sample_datasets.py)
from `app/irt/simulate.py`, from parameters that are written down, with a seed
that is recorded. So for each file the right answer is a known quantity, and you
can check what the estimator returns against what generated it.

The parameters are in the sibling `<name>.parameters.csv`. `MANIFEST.json` holds
the seeds, shapes and SHA-256 of each CSV, and `backend/tests/test_sample_datasets.py`
checks the shipped files against those digests — so if one of these CSVs is
edited by hand, the suite fails rather than letting its documented parameters
quietly stop describing it.

These are simulated data. They exercise the platform; they do not validate it.
The estimator's correctness is the job of `backend/tests/validation/` (parameter
recovery over all seven families, and agreement with R `mirt`).

## The files

| File | Respondents | Items | Responses | Generated from | Seed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dichotomous_small.csv` | 200 | 20 | 0/1 | 2PL | 20260803 |
| `dichotomous_medium.csv` | 500 | 30 | 0/1 | 3PL | 20260804 |
| `polytomous_likert.csv` | 300 | 15 | 1–5, 6% missing | GRM | 20260805 |
| `dichotomous_dif.csv` | 800 | 20 | 0/1 | 2PL, two groups | 20260806 / 20260807 |

In all four, item discriminations are drawn from U(0.8, 2.0) and difficulties are
spread evenly from −2 to +2 (GRM: category boundaries centred on −1.5 to +1.5,
0.67 logits apart). Abilities are drawn from a standard normal.

### `dichotomous_small.csv`

The one to upload first: 200 × 20 fits in seconds. At that sample size the
standard errors are wide enough to be worth reading. Fitting the generating 2PL
recovers difficulty well and discrimination only roughly — correlations with the
truth of 0.98 and 0.77, RMSE 0.32 and 0.28. Item fit statistics that come back
non-significant here are as likely to be short of power as to be evidence of fit.

### `dichotomous_medium.csv`

Generated with real lower asymptotes, c between 0.15 and 0.25, so a 3PL fit has
something to find. It will not find it. Fitting the generating 3PL recovers
difficulty (r = 0.99) and discrimination (r = 0.71) but carries essentially no
information about the lower asymptote: across the 30 items, estimated c
correlates with true c at −0.01. The Beta(5, 17) prior is doing the work, which
is what it is for.

That is a property of the design, not a defect in the estimator — a lower
asymptote is identified by respondents far below the item's difficulty, and at
n = 500 there are few of them per item. It is also why this file is not
described as "suitable for the 3PL". Treat a c estimate from it as a prior with
data-shaped noise on top, and see the note on M2's power against guessing in
`PROGRESS.md`.

### `polytomous_likert.csv`

A five-point rating scale coded 1–5, the way survey data actually arrives, with
6% of responses missing completely at random. Two things to watch in the
validation record: it reports shifting the codes to 0-based without claiming any
category was removed, and it reports the missing rate and states that each
respondent contributes the items they answered with nothing imputed. Fitting the
generating GRM recovers the category boundaries at r = 0.99 (RMSE 0.15) and the
discriminations at r = 0.91.

### `dichotomous_dif.csv`

The example for DIF screening, and the only file with `respondent_id` and
`group` columns — so it is also the one to use when trying out the id and
grouping column declarations on upload. 400 respondents in group `reference`,
400 in `focal`.

Items 5, 11 and 17 are 0.8 logits harder for the focal group. Nothing else
differs: both groups are drawn from the same trait distribution, so there is no
impact to confound the observed-score methods. This is deliberately the best
case for Mantel-Haenszel rather than a representative one — real group
differences in the trait are the usual reason MH and the logistic method
disagree.

Screening a pooled 2PL fit over `group` flags exactly those three items and
nothing else, on all three methods, after Benjamini-Hochberg: MH χ² of 47.7,
14.1 and 15.3 (ETS C, B and C), with adjusted p-values below 0.005 from the
logistic and IRT likelihood-ratio methods too.

This file is complete, with no missing responses, and that is not an oversight.
The observed-score methods need a matching score, which is undefined for a
respondent who left part of the test blank, so they use complete cases. An
earlier draft carried 8% missing spread over 20 items — which leaves 140
complete cases out of 800, below the per-group minimum, so the dataset whose
whole purpose is DIF produced no DIF statistics at all. Missing-data handling is
demonstrated by `polytomous_likert.csv` instead.

## Regenerating

```bash
cd backend
python scripts/generate_sample_datasets.py
```

This rewrites the CSVs, the parameter files and `MANIFEST.json`. Output is
byte-identical for a given numpy: the draws come from explicitly seeded PCG64
streams. Change a spec in the script and the digests change with it, which is
the intended way to alter one of these datasets.

## A note on what these are for

Uploading `dichotomous_small.csv` and requesting several models will produce a
comparison dossier that declines to name a best model — including, quite
possibly, a verdict that the candidates are statistically indistinguishable
while a likelihood-ratio test between them is significant. That is not a
contradiction, and `docs/explainers/part11-worked-example.html` walks through
why on a run of this kind.
